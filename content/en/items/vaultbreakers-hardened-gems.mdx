One hardened gem can decide whether your run ends in a clean extraction or a long walk back to the same vault door. That is the entire appeal of the item chase: gems are the fastest way to change how a build feels, and hardened gems are the version of that power you can actually rely on. If you have been stacking raw stones and wondering why your damage plateaus halfway through a tier, Vaultbreakers hardened gems are almost certainly the missing piece. This guide covers what they are, where to farm them, how the hardening process works, and which ones deserve your stash space.

## What Are Vaultbreakers Hardened Gems?

Every gem you pull out of a vault starts life as a raw, unstable stone. It rolls somewhere inside a wide stat band, it is cheap to replace, and it is easy to treat as filler. Hardening changes that. You spend materials and currency to lock the roll, push the numbers toward the top of their range, and turn a disposable pickup into a permanent part of your build.

The practical difference is commitment. Raw gems are what you gamble with while leveling or testing an affix. Hardened gems are what you socket when you have decided how the character is supposed to work.

| Aspect | Raw gems | Hardened gems |
|---|---|---|
| Main source | Common vault chests, enemy drops, breakables | Hardening stations, high-tier vault rewards, trade |
| Stat quality | Wide, unpredictable roll ranges | Tightened roll pushed toward the high end |
| Build impact | Minor and temporary | Build-defining and long-term |
| Investment | Almost none | Materials, currency, and time |
| Best used for | Testing affixes, early leveling | Locking in a finished build |
| Trade value | Low, sold in bulk | High, often your most valuable inventory item |

A useful rule of thumb from player experience: if you would not notice a gem being deleted from your stash, it is not worth hardening yet.

## Where to Farm Hardened Gems

You rarely farm hardened gems directly. The realistic loop is farming raw gems and hardening materials, then converting them at a station. That means your farming plan should target volume first and quality second, because you need a lot of rolls before a top-band gem appears.

| Source | Difficulty | Typical volume | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-tier vault runs | Low | High | Stockpiling raw gems and basic materials |
| High-tier vaults | High | Medium | Best odds at top-band rolls |
| Boss and elite caches | Medium to high | Low | Targeted affix families |
| Daily and weekly contracts | Low | Medium | Steady material income |
| Player trade or market | Varies | Varies | Filling one specific slot fast |
| Salvaging unused gems | None | High | Recycling junk into hardening materials |

A few habits separate efficient farmers from players who plateau:

- **Clear the highest tier you can finish without dying.** A completed high-tier run beats three failed low-tier attempts every time.
- **Sweep everything.** Breakables and side rooms are where bulk raw gems hide, and bulk is what feeds the hardening loop.
- **Group up when you can.** Community reports consistently suggest that coordinated groups extract more raw gems per hour than solo players, even after splitting loot.
- **Bank before you harden.** Never carry your entire gem stock into a vault you are not certain you can clear.

## The Hardening Process, Step by Step

The exact names of materials rotate between patches, but the underlying loop has stayed consistent. These are the steps that matter.

1. **Bank your raw gems.** Move everything out of your run inventory before you start crafting. Losing an unhardened gem to a failed extraction is annoying; losing a stack of them is a session-ending mistake.
2. **Sort by affix family, not by rarity color.** Rarity tells you how loud a gem looks in your inventory. The affix tells you whether it does anything for your build.
3. **Check the roll band.** Compare the current roll against the possible range. A gem sitting near the bottom of its band will still be mediocre after hardening.
4. **Gather the inputs.** You need the raw gem, a hardening material, and the station fee. Optional reroll items let you push a roll further, but they are a luxury, not a requirement.
5. **Harden at the station.** Confirm the gem and pay the cost. Treat this as a commitment, not a test.
6. **Socket it and run a real vault.** A gem that looks good on paper can feel completely different in combat. Test before you harden the next one.

| Input | Role in the process | Practical tip |
|---|---|---|
| Raw gem | The base item being upgraded | Only harden gems from the top of their roll band |
| Hardening material | The main consumable cost | Farm it passively through contracts and salvaging |
| Station fee | Currency sink | Budget for it; fees add up fast across a full set |
| Reroll item (optional) | Pushes an existing roll higher | Save these for gems already in your final build |
| Socket slot | Where the gem actually lives | Plan your slots before you spend anything |

## Best Hardened Gems by Build Archetype

Gem names and numbers shift with patches, so it is more useful to think in terms of affix families. The table below maps the archetypes most players run to the affix priorities that actually move the needle.

| Archetype | Priority affixes | Useful secondary | Usually a waste |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melee bruiser | Damage reduction, life on hit | Attack speed, stagger resistance | Pure crit stacking |
| Ranged crit | Crit chance, crit damage | Projectile speed, penetration | Thorns and reflect |
| Summoner | Minion damage, minion survivability | Cooldown reduction | Personal weapon damage |
| Support or utility | Cooldown reduction, area size | Movement speed | Raw damage multipliers |
| Glass cannon | Damage multipliers, penetration | Mobility, dodge | Defensive filler stats |

One pattern shows up again and again in community reports: three well-chosen hardened gems outperform eight mediocre ones. Socket space is limited, so every slot you spend on a filler affix is a slot you are not spending on your build's actual win condition.

## Mistakes That Waste Vaultbreakers Hardened Gems

Most wasted gems are not lost to bad luck. They are lost to a handful of avoidable decisions.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hardening a low-band roll | You pay full price for a mediocre result | Only harden gems near the top of their range |
| Chasing rarity color | Rarity does not equal fit | Sort by affix family instead |
| Hoarding every gem | Your stash fills while your build stays weak | Harden and slot, or sell what you will not use |
| Ignoring socket limits | You craft gems you cannot equip | Count your slots before you spend materials |
| Re-hardening on a whim | Drains materials for no real gain | Test a build for a full session before committing |
| Vendoring instead of trading | You leave value on the table | Check the market for top-band gems first |

Two more tips worth internalizing:

- **Hardened gems are a build decision, not a loot decision.** Decide what the character does first, then farm the gems that support it.
- **Keep one backup set.** A spare hardened gem for your main affix means a bad patch or a failed experiment does not set you back a week.

## FAQ

**What are Vaultbreakers hardened gems used for?**
They are the upgraded, committed version of the raw gems you find in vaults. You socket them into your build to lock in a strong stat roll, and they form the core of most endgame setups. Raw gems are for testing; hardened gems are for keeping.

**Can you lose hardened gems when you die in a vault?**
Risk rules vary by mode and by patch, so check the current in-game rules before you carry anything valuable. The safe habit that community reports keep recommending is simple: bank your gems before a run you are not confident about, and only carry what you can afford to lose.

**Should you harden gems early or wait until endgame?**
Early on, raw gems are usually enough to clear content. Save your hardening materials for gems that sit near the top of their roll band and match an affix your build actually wants. Spending materials on filler gems is the single most common early-game regret.

**Are hardened gems better than a gear upgrade?**
It depends on which slot is weaker. If your weapon or armor is far behind your gem quality, fix the gear first. If your gear is solid and your gems are still raw, hardening is usually the cheaper and faster power spike available to you.

Get the loop right — farm volume, sort by affix, harden only top-band rolls, and test before you commit — and hardened gems stop being a gamble and start being the most reliable progression system in the game.
