## The Core Loop Every Vaultbreakers Run Follows

Every run, whether you're solo-queuing or running a full squad, moves through the same broad phases. Players who treat these phases as separate decisions rather than one continuous blur tend to extract far more often. Community reports consistently point to the same thing: squads that lose track of *which* phase they're in are the squads that get caught flat-footed.

| Phase | What You're Doing | Pressure Level | Most Common Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | Choosing loadout, roles, and a primary objective | Low | Overloading on gear you can't afford to lose |
| Breach | Entering the vault and clearing immediate threats | Rising | Splitting up before the entry is secure |
| Secure | Grabbing loot, completing objectives, holding space | High | Greed — staying past the point of good returns |
| Extract | Leaving with what you have | Peak | No agreed exit route or fallback |
| Reset | Stashing loot, reviewing the run, adjusting | None | Repeating the same mistake next run |

The single most valuable habit is deciding your exit condition *before* you breach. That might be a loot value, a specific objective, or a signal from your scout. Once that condition is met, you leave. No debate, no "one more room."

### Why the Exit Plan Comes First

Extraction games punish hesitation asymmetrically. Staying an extra ninety seconds can multiply your haul, but it can also multiply your risk of running into a fresher, better-positioned squad. Setting a hard exit trigger removes that decision from the heat of the moment, when adrenaline tends to argue for staying. Veteran players describe this as "playing to leave" rather than "playing to win" — the goal isn't clearing the map, it's banking progress.

## Squad Roles and Team Composition That Actually Work

Vaultbreakers rewards role clarity more than raw individual skill. You don't need a rigid class system — you need each player to know what they're responsible for when contact happens. The archetypes below show up in nearly every successful composition, though the exact names and tools vary by build and by how your group likes to play.

| Role | Primary Job | Tools That Help | Skill to Practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breacher | Open the entry and create the first safe angle | Breach tools, shields, entry utility | Timing your push with your squad |
| Scout | Find threats, call rotations, read the map | Mobility items, recon tools | Clear, calm callouts |
| Support | Keep the squad alive and supplied | Healing, revives, ammo or utility drops | Positioning behind the front line |
| Anchor | Hold space and cover the retreat | Defensive gear, area denial | Patience — not chasing kills |
| Hauler | Carry the objective and high-value loot | Extra storage, escape tools | Knowing when to drop and run |

For smaller groups, the Anchor and Hauler duties usually merge, and the Scout often doubles as the Breacher. Player experience suggests that a two-person squad works best when one player leads and one covers — trying to split three roles across two people rarely ends well.

### Communication Beats Composition

You can win with an unusual team as long as everyone talks. Use short, directional callouts: location first, threat second, action third. "Upper walkway, two contacts, holding" tells your squad more in four seconds than a paragraph of panic ever will. Mute-friendly ping systems help, but voice still wins when things get chaotic.

## Loadout and Loot Priorities for New and Returning Players

New players tend to over-invest in damage and under-invest in escape. The uncomfortable truth is that most failed runs end because someone couldn't get out, not because they couldn't fight. Build your loadout around surviving the trip home.

| Slot Priority | What to Bring | Why It Matters | Skip Early On |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Breach | A reliable way through doors and barriers | Every run starts with an entry problem | Exotic breaching gear with high cost |
| 2 — Escape | Mobility or smoke-style cover | Turns a losing fight into a clean exit | Damage boosts |
| 3 — Sustain | Healing and at least one revive option | Keeps a bad moment from becoming a wipe | Stacking three of the same item |
| 4 — Utility | Information or area denial | Lets you fight on your terms | Situational gadgets you never use |
| 5 — Flex | One slot you leave open | Loot you find mid-run goes here | Filling every slot during prep |

### Inventory Discipline

Leave a slot or two open. Squads that fill every pocket before breaching end up leaving valuable finds behind or, worse, standing still while they decide what to drop. A simple rule works well: if an item doesn't clearly beat something you already carry, don't pick it up. Sell value beats hoard value.

## Vault Selection, Map Awareness, and Extraction Timing

Not every vault deserves your time. The best squads rank targets before they commit, based on how contested the area is, how well they know the layout, and how many exits they can reach quickly.

| Vault Tier | Risk Profile | Best Suited For | Recommended Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry tier | Low contest, modest rewards | Learning layouts and mechanics | Complete one objective and leave |
| Mid tier | Moderate contest, solid loot | Squads with a few successful runs behind them | Two objectives, then extract |
| High tier | Heavy contest, high value | Coordinated groups with clear roles | One objective, fast exit |
| Contested / event | Unpredictable, third parties likely | Confident squads chasing big payouts | Extract early or avoid entirely |

Map awareness is a skill you build deliberately. On each run, pick one thing to learn: a rotation route, a sightline, a quiet entrance, or a fallback position. After a handful of runs you'll have a mental map of where fights start and where they end — and that's worth more than any single piece of gear.

Timing matters just as much. Extraction windows tighten as a match progresses, and late rotations are where squads get pinched between two other groups. If your objective is done and your exit is clear, take it. A completed run beats a perfect one.

## Common Mistakes and a Practice Plan to Fix Them

Most wipes come from a small set of repeatable errors. Fixing two or three of these will improve your results faster than any gear upgrade.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Splitting up without a plan | Isolated players get picked off one at a time | Agree on a regroup point before every push |
| Looting mid-fight | You become a stationary target | Clear the area, then loot |
| Ignoring audio cues | You lose the first three seconds of every fight | Play a session with music off and listen |
| Hoarding consumables | You die with full pockets | Use items when they save a run |
| No fallback route | One blocked exit becomes a wipe | Identify two exits before you breach |
| Chasing kills | Fights cost resources and time | Fight to escape, not to clear |

### A Simple Weekly Routine

| Practice Block | Focus | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up run | Mechanics | Cheap loadout, no looting goals, just move and shoot |
| Role run | Consistency | Play the same role for several runs in a row |
| Review | Decision-making | Watch one failed run and name the moment it went wrong |
| Stretch run | Risk management | Take a higher-tier vault with a hard exit condition |

## FAQ

**What should a beginner focus on first?**
Movement, exits, and sound. Damage comes later. Players who learn the maps and the extraction routes early progress faster than players who chase fights, because they survive long enough to keep their loot.

**Do you need a premade squad to do well?**
No, but communication matters more than the squad. If you're playing with random teammates, use pings, follow the group's lead player, and never wander off alone without telling anyone.

**How do you decide when to extract?**
Set the condition before you breach — a loot value, an objective, or a teammate's call. Once it's met, leave. The most profitable run is the one that ends on your terms.

**How often should you change your loadout?**
Change one variable at a time. Swap a single slot, run a few games, and see whether your survival rate improves. Wholesale rebuilds make it impossible to tell what actually helped.

Stick to the loop, respect the exit, and treat every run as practice for the next one. That's how a Vaultbreakers guide turns into a winning habit.
