## What "Extraction" Means in Vaultbreakers

Vaultbreakers extraction is the moment a run stops being a scavenger hunt and becomes a decision. Everything you pick up — gear, materials, objective items — only counts once it's in your stash, and the map is full of players who would rather take your haul than find their own. That's why the Vaultbreakers extraction loop, not the loot table, is the real skill check. A run you don't bank is worth nothing, no matter how good the drops were.

In extraction-style games, a match is a round trip rather than a last-one-standing contest. You deploy with equipment you already own, you search for value, and at some point you have to walk to an exit while other players are doing the same thing with the same greed. The tension comes from the fact that leaving early feels like quitting, and leaving late feels like gambling.

If you're coming from other shooters, the mental shift is bigger than it sounds. Here's how the win condition differs.

| Mode style | What "winning" means | What you keep afterward |
|---|---|---|
| Battle royale | Be the last player or team alive | Match-based rewards only |
| Team deathmatch | Reach the score or time limit first | Nothing from the map |
| Wave defense | Survive as many rounds as possible | Partial, performance-based rewards |
| Extraction (Vaultbreakers) | Reach an exit alive with your haul | Everything you carried out |

That last row is the whole game. Your stash is the scoreboard, and the only way to move it is to survive the trip home. ## The Vaultbreakers Extraction Loop, Step by Step

Every run follows roughly the same arc, even when the map, mode, and squad size change. Understanding the arc lets you plan two steps ahead instead of reacting to whatever noise you hear first.

| Phase | What's happening | The decision in front of you |
|---|---|---|
| Prep and loadout | You choose gear from your stash | How much am I willing to lose this run? |
| Insertion | You spawn with a time budget and a map | Which loot zone is worth the walk? |
| Looting and objectives | You fill inventory and finish tasks | Do I clear one more room or start moving? |
| Pressure | Players, hazards, or AI close in | Fight, hide, or rotate toward an exit? |
| Extraction window | An exit is live, contested, or closing | Board now, or gamble on a second sweep? |
| Debrief | Items bank, gear is kept or lost | What do I change next run? |

### The mistake almost everyone makes first

New players treat insertion as the start of the run. Experienced players treat it as the middle. Before you load in, you should already know which exit you're aiming for and roughly what route gets you there. That single habit fixes most "I had great loot and died at the door" stories.

### Why the pressure phase decides your run

Most losses don't happen because someone out-aimed you. They happen because you were standing in a room with a full inventory and no plan. Once your bag is full, your goal changes from "find more" to "protect what I have." Player experience suggests that the fastest improvement most people see comes from leaving earlier than feels comfortable.

## Loadouts, Loot Priority, and Inventory Discipline

Gear fear is the most common reason players stall out. They hoard good equipment, run budget kits forever, and never learn how the good gear actually handles. A healthier approach is a three-kit rule: one cheap kit you don't mind losing, one mid-tier kit for serious runs, and one premium kit you take out when you have a specific objective in mind.

| Kit type | Best for | Cost of losing it |
|---|---|---|
| Budget kit | Learning routes, warm-up runs, solo scouting | Low — replaceable from common loot |
| Mid-tier kit | Standard runs, duo play, contested objectives | Moderate — a few runs to rebuild |
| Premium kit | High-value objectives, coordinated squads | High — painful but not run-ending |
| Utility kit | Support play, revives, area control | Low to moderate — value comes from teamwork |

### Loot priority: what goes in the bag first

Inventory space is the real currency of any Vaultbreakers extraction run, so decide your priority order before you're standing over three containers with ten seconds of quiet left.

| Priority | Loot type | Why it ranks here | When to drop it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Objective and quest items | Progress that can't be bought | Almost never |
| 2 | Compact high-value items | Best value per inventory slot | Only to make room for more value |
| 3 | Crafting materials | Steady long-term income | When slots get tight |
| 4 | Ammo and medical supplies | Keeps you alive to extract | When you're overstocked |
| 5 | Gear taken from defeated players | High value, high risk to carry | When it slows your exit |

### Inventory discipline in practice

- Fill your bag in layers: essentials first, value second, bulk last.
- Ask "would I die for this item?" before you take a fight over it.
- Keep one slot free for something better than what you already have.
- Learn which containers are worth the search time and which are noise.
- Bank often. A small extraction that succeeds beats a big one that doesn't.

## Timing, Exits, and Third-Party Pressure

Extraction points are magnets. They attract players who want out, players who want to ambush people who want out, and players who just happen to be passing through. Reading the window correctly is the difference between a clean exit and a firefight at the door.

| Window state | Risk level | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| Early window | Low traffic, more time | Good for damaged squads or small hauls |
| Mid window | Moderate, other players rotating | Move with cover, avoid open ground |
| Contested window | High, likely contact | Wait for the fight to resolve, then move |
| Late or closing window | Highest pressure, no second chance | Commit fully, use cover and smokes |

Third-party pressure is the mechanic that catches most teams. Two squads trade shots, one wins, and a third group walks in to collect the leftovers. If you hear a fight near an exit, you have three options: avoid it entirely, wait for it to finish and then move fast, or push in immediately while everyone is distracted. The right call depends on your health, your ammo, and how much you're carrying.

Community reports consistently point to the same pattern: the players who extract most often are not the ones with the best aim, they're the ones who treat the exit as the objective from the first minute.

## Solo vs. Squad, Plus Common Mistakes to Fix

Solo and group play reward completely different habits. Solo, you are your own scout, medic, and getaway driver. In a squad, you can specialize — but you also have to communicate, because a team that loots in four directions extracts in none.

| Situation | Solo approach | Squad approach |
|---|---|---|
| Contested loot zone | Loot the edges, avoid the center | Split coverage, one player watches |
| Taking contact | Break line of sight and disengage | Trade damage, flank, or suppress |
| Player down | Avoid the fight entirely | Revive only with cover and a plan |
| Full inventory | Extract immediately | Nominate a runner to bank the haul |
| Loud extraction | Pick a quieter exit instead | Cover the exit before boarding |

### Common mistakes and their fixes

- **Looting past full.** Fix: leave one slot open and set a hard cap on your run.
- **Fighting over scraps.** Fix: only take a fight that protects something valuable.
- **Ignoring the clock.** Fix: check your exit window every few minutes, not at the end.
- **Running premium gear with no plan.** Fix: match the kit to the objective.
- **Extracting in a straight line.** Fix: use cover, elevation, and odd angles on approach.

The Vaultbreakers extraction mindset is simple: everything before the exit is setup, and everything after it is profit.

## FAQ

**Is Vaultbreakers extraction the same as a battle royale circle?**
Not quite. A battle royale forces everyone toward one closing area. In Vaultbreakers extraction, exits are choices you make, and the danger comes from other players making the same choice at the same time.

**What's the safest way to extract with a full inventory?**
Leave earlier than you want to, take a less popular exit, and avoid fights near the extraction point. Player experience suggests that a slightly smaller haul you actually bank is worth more than a big one you lose.

**Should I run my best gear or budget kits?**
Use a mix. Budget kits let you learn routes cheaply, while mid-tier and premium kits let you contest better objectives. Rotate based on what you're trying to accomplish.

**How do I stop losing loot at the door?**
Approach exits with cover, watch for third parties, and never sprint into an open extraction zone just because the timer is short. The final thirty seconds of a Vaultbreakers extraction run are where most hauls are lost — treat them like the most dangerous part of the map, because they usually are.
